Home / What we do

We move sports equipment from where it sits idle to where it is needed.

Four steps, no middlemen, nothing wasted. Here is exactly what happens between your cupboard and a child’s hands.

The process

How it actually works.

We collect

Equipment reaches us in three ways: you drop it at a collection point, we arrange a pickup, or a school, club or housing society runs a drive and we come and take the lot. Every item is logged when it arrives — what it is, what condition it is in, and who gave it.

We assess

Each piece is graded into one of three piles. Ready to play goes straight to the distribution shelf. Needs work goes to a partner store. Beyond repair is stripped for usable parts and the rest is sent for recycling. We would rather turn something down than pass on equipment that could injure a child.

We refurbish

We partner with neighbourhood sports stores rather than doing repairs in-house. They already have the tools, the materials and years of practice at re-gripping bats, restringing rackets and restitching balls. The work is done properly, and the money and skill stay in the same neighbourhood.

We redistribute

Refurbished gear goes to government schools and to academies coaching young athletes who have the ability but not the equipment. We hand it over in person, record what went where, and stay in touch with the school so we know whether it is actually being used.

Who receives it

We give to people who will use it, not store it.

Equipment only helps if someone plays with it. Before we hand anything over we want to know who will look after it, how many children will use it, and whether there is a coach or teacher who will actually run sessions.

Government schools

Where a PE period exists on the timetable but the equipment cupboard is empty. We work through the headmaster or the sports teacher.

Academies & young talent

Coaches often spot a promising player who simply cannot afford their own kit. We supply the individual through the academy.

Community & NGO programmes

Organisations already running regular sport for children in under-resourced areas.

Our standards

Rules we do not bend.

Safety over volume

Helmets, pads and anything protective are checked hardest. If we cannot be confident it will protect a child, it does not go out. Ever.

Nothing is sold

Donated equipment is never resold, by us or by anyone we give it to. It is given, free, to the children who will play with it.

Dignity, not charity

Handovers are not photo opportunities. We never publish a child’s face or name without written permission from the school and the family.
