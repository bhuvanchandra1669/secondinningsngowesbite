Equipment is only half of it. We also need hands, workshop benches, storage space and people who will make an introduction.

Corporate & CSR

Office drives, sponsored refurbishment, and circular-economy reporting.

Volunteer

Hands, mostly on weekends.

Most of our work happens in short bursts — a collection day, a sorting afternoon, a handover at a school. You do not need to commit to anything ongoing.

If you can drive, carry, sort, photograph, write, or simply give up a Saturday, there is something for you.

Collection & transport

Picking equipment up and getting it to the sorting point. A vehicle helps but is not required.

Sorting & grading

Going through what has come in and deciding what is ready, what needs work, and what cannot be saved.

Photography & social

Documenting handovers and keeping our Instagram alive. Genuinely one of the most useful things you can do.

Outreach

Talking to schools, academies and stores. Good if you are comfortable making the first call.

Sign up to volunteer

Collection drives

The fastest way to help a lot at once.

A single school drive can bring in more equipment than a month of individual donations. It takes about two weeks to organise and very little of your time.

Tell us you want to run one

Fill in the form below with roughly how many people you can reach and when you would like to run it.

We send you the kit

Posters, a collection box sign, a short explainer for a WhatsApp group, and social media graphics. Everything is ready to print or forward.

You collect for a week or two

A box in the reception, the staff room or the club house. We will tell you what to accept and what to turn away.

We come and take the lot

One pickup at the end, and we send you the totals afterwards so you can tell your community what they achieved.

Sports stores

If you repair equipment, you are the missing piece.

We deliberately do not do repairs in-house. Re-gripping a bat, restringing a racket, restitching a ball — these are skills that already exist in every neighbourhood, and they belong to you.

Partner stores take in batches of equipment, bring them back to a playable standard, and are credited on this site and in every handover we do. Where we can, we cover materials.

Give funds

Money goes further on repairs than on new kit.

A new cricket bat costs thousands. Re-gripping one that already exists costs a fraction of that, and puts the same bat in the same child’s hands. That gap is the whole reason we exist — and it is where any money you give us goes.

We do not spend donations on salaries or offices, because we do not have either. It goes to the partner stores doing the repairs, and to the fuel and packaging that gets equipment across the city.

Please read this first. Second Innings is a registered organisation but does not yet hold 80G certification. That means we cannot issue a tax-deductible receipt for money you give us. We will issue a plain acknowledgement, and we will tell you exactly what your contribution paid for. If a tax receipt matters to you, please give us equipment or time instead — both are worth just as much to us.

Add your real figures here once you have a few invoices from the partner stores — for example what a re-grip, a restring and a re-stitch actually cost you. Naming the real number is far more persuasive than asking for “any amount”.

How to send it

Add your UPI ID and bank details here once the organisation account is open. Until then, get in touch and we will send them to you directly.

What you get back

An acknowledgement, and a note telling you which items your money repaired and where they went. Not a tax receipt — see the note opposite.

Space & transport

Our real bottleneck is not gear. It is somewhere to put it.

Equipment arrives in bursts — a school drive can produce sixty items in an afternoon. It then needs somewhere dry to sit while it is graded, sent for repair, and matched to a school.

If you have a spare room, a garage, an unused shop back-room, or a vehicle you could lend on a weekend, that is genuinely one of the most useful things anyone can offer us.

Offer space or a vehicle

Storage

Anything dry and lockable. Even a few square metres changes what we can accept in one go.

A vehicle, occasionally

Most pickups are one car boot. A driver for a Saturday morning is often all a drive needs.

A workbench

Somewhere volunteers can sit and sort. A garage or a spare classroom works perfectly.

Companies

A CSR project that is easy to photograph and easy to measure.

What we do sits neatly across two things companies are already reporting on: community sport and the circular economy. Every item we rescue is an item that did not become waste.

We can run an office collection drive, take sponsorship for a batch of refurbishments, or arrange a volunteering day for your team at a sorting session or a school handover.

Please note: Second Innings does not currently hold 80G certification, so we cannot issue tax-deductible receipts for financial contributions. Equipment, materials, logistics and volunteering time are all things we can accept today.

Office collection drive

We provide the materials, you provide the cupboard. Usually two weeks.

Sponsor a refurbishment batch

Cover the repair cost for a set number of items and we will report back on exactly where they went.

Team volunteering day

A sorting session or a school handover. More useful than it sounds, and rather more memorable than a workshop.
