A second life for sports equipment

Every bat deserves a second innings.

Cricket bats, footballs and rackets sit unused in cupboards while thousands of children have the talent but not the kit. We collect the first, refurbish it, and put it in the hands of the second — from everywhere in Hyderabad.

Why we exist

Talent is spread evenly. Equipment is not.

A cricket bat costs more than a month of groceries for many families. A decent pair of football boots can decide whether a child ever gets picked for a team. The gap is rarely talent — it is almost always kit.

Meanwhile, perfectly good equipment is quietly retiring in homes across the country. Outgrown, upgraded, or simply forgotten. Our mission is to make sport accessible to everyone by moving that equipment from where it is idle to where it is needed most.

The journey of a bat

From the back of a cupboard to the middle of a pitch.

  • Collected
  • Assessed
  • Refurbished
  • Playing again
01 — Collected

It starts in a cupboard.

Somewhere in your home there is a bat you outgrew, a racket from a phase that passed, a football nobody kicks any more. You drop it at a collection point, or we come and pick it up.

We take gear for cricket, football, badminton, basketball, tennis, table tennis, padel, squash, pickleball, hockey, volleyball, athletics, swimming, gymnastics, cycling, boxing, skating and kabaddi.

02 — Assessed

Every piece gets checked.

Not everything can be saved, and safety comes before volume. We grade each item into one of three piles: ready to play, needs work, or beyond repair. Nothing unsafe ever reaches a child.

Anything genuinely beyond repair is sent for recycling — never landfill.

03 — Refurbished

Local shops bring it back.

We partner with neighbourhood sports stores — the people who already know how to re-grip a bat, re-string a racket and re-stitch a ball. The repairs happen locally, by the people who do it best.

Working with local stores keeps both the money and the skill in the same neighbourhood.

04 — Playing again

Back where it belongs.

Refurbished gear goes to government schools and to young athletes in academies who have the ability but not the equipment. A bat that spent three years in a cupboard spends the next three in a net.

Wherever we can, we send the original donor a photo of their gear in its new hands.

What we accept

If a child can play with it, we want it.

Bats, balls, rackets, pads, gloves, boots, nets, cones. Used is fine. Scuffed is fine. Broken beyond repair is not — but if you are unsure, send us a photo and we will tell you.
