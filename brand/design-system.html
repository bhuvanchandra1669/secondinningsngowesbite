01 — Logo

The mark.

The circular mark is the primary identity. It works on its own once people know us; until then, pair it with the wordmark. There are three supplied files, all with a transparent background.

mark-512.png
Primary, on light

On dark

Minimum size
24 px / 8 mm

Do

Keep clear space around the mark equal to the width of the football inside it. Place it on cream, white or a cream circle. Use the transparent PNG, never a screenshot.

Don't

Don't recolour it, add a drop shadow, stretch it, rotate it, put it on a busy photo, or drop it straight onto navy without the cream circle — the football's white panels disappear.

02 — Colour

Navy carries it. Orange points at things.

Navy is the voice; orange is the finger. Use orange only for the one thing you want tapped — a button, a link, a number. If two things on a layout are orange, one of them is wrong.

Core

Navy 800#0A2740Dark sections, headings on light
Navy 600#134063Body headings, logo wordmark
Orange 500#E1601FPrimary action. Use sparingly

Accents — from the logo

Amber 400#F2A81DHighlights on dark
Green 400#3E9E44Positive / accepted
Blue 500#1C6BA8Secondary accent

Neutrals

Cream#FBF9F6Page background. Never pure white
Cream 2#F4F0EAAlternate section bands
Ink#0A1E2EBody text

Rough proportions: 60% cream, 25% navy, 10% neutral greys, 5% orange. Contrast: Ink on Cream and Cream on Navy 800 both pass WCAG AA. Orange 500 on cream passes for large text only — never set body copy in orange.

03 — Typography

Two fonts. Both free.

Fraunces for anything large and emotional. Inter for anything you have to read. Both are on Google Fonts, free for commercial use, and available in Canva.

Every bat deserves Display / H1 Fraunces · 500 30pt / 1.04 tracking -0.022em Hero headlines only. One per screen.
Small numbers, honestly counted Heading / H2 Fraunces · 500 20pt / 1.08 tracking -0.022em Section headings.
Back where it belongs Heading / H3 Fraunces · 500 13pt / 1.2 tracking -0.015em Card and block titles.
A cricket bat costs more than a month of groceries. Lead Inter · 400 11pt / 1.6 tracking 0 Intro paragraph under a heading.
We collect from everywhere in Hyderabad. Body Inter · 400 9.5pt / 1.65 tracking 0 Default running text.
WHAT WE ACCEPT Eyebrow Inter · 600 8pt / 1.4 tracking 0.18em Always uppercase, always orange.
Donate your gear Button Inter · 600 9pt / 1 tracking 0.005em Sentence case, never ALL CAPS.

The one rule

Fraunces never goes below 13pt and never carries a full paragraph. Inter never carries a hero headline. Mixing them the other way round is what makes a layout look homemade.

If Fraunces is unavailable

Substitute Playfair Display or Instrument Serif. For Inter, substitute DM Sans or system default. Never Times or Arial.

04 — Icons

Eighteen sports, one pen.

All icons are drawn on a 24×24 grid with a 1.5 px stroke, round caps and round joins, and no fill. That single rule is what makes them look like a set. If you add a sport, match it exactly.

07 — Voice

Plain, specific, never pitiful.

We are matching supply with demand, not begging. The tone is a capable friend explaining something simple — warm, concrete and slightly understated. Understatement is what makes people believe you.

Sounds like us

“That bat is not doing anything in there.”

“63 items, 4 schools. Small numbers, honestly counted.”

“Talent is spread evenly. Equipment is not.”

“We collect from everywhere in Hyderabad.”

Doesn't

“Help these poor underprivileged children!”

“Empowering youth through the transformative power of sport.”

“Thousands of lives changed!!”

“Donate now — every rupee counts!”

Four habits

Name the number

“Six footballs to one school” beats “many items to many schools”. If the number is small, say the small number.

Never invent impact

One made-up figure a funder catches discredits everything else on the page. Where something is an estimate, say so.

Dignity over pity

Describe children as players, not victims. They are not lucky to receive; the equipment is lucky to be used.

Short sentences

If a sentence needs a comma to survive, cut it in two. Read it aloud — if you run out of breath, it is too long.
